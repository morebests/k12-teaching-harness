# Grade 8 Unit 5: Linear Functions and Contextual Modeling
## Full 14-Period Unit Skeleton and Target-Evidence Architecture (Candidate Version 2.0 — Cross-Layer Budget Revision)

**Target Grade:** Grade 8 (US Public School Curriculum)  
**Unit Duration:** Exactly 14 Regular Periods (50 Minutes each)  
*(Revised from 18 periods to 14 periods in accordance with the 20% budget reduction directive: $\lfloor 18 \times 0.8 \rfloor = \lfloor 14.4 \rfloor = 14$ periods).*  
**Primary CCSS Content Standards:** 
- **8.F.B.4:** Construct a function to model a linear relationship between two quantities. Determine the rate of change and initial value of the function from a description of a relationship or from two $(x, y)$ values, including reading these from a table or from a graph. Interpret the rate of change and initial value of a linear function in terms of the situation it models, and in terms of its graph or a table of values.
- **8.F.B.5:** Describe qualitatively the functional relationship between two quantities by analyzing a graph (e.g., where the function is increasing or decreasing, linear or nonlinear). Sketch a graph that exhibits the qualitative features of a function that has been described verbally.
**Integrated Standards for Mathematical Practice:** MP1 (Problem solving & perseverance), MP2 (Quantitative & abstract reasoning), MP3 (Construct viable arguments & critique reasoning), MP4 (Mathematical modeling), MP5 (Strategic tool use), MP6 (Precision & units), MP7 (Making use of structure).  
**Classroom Operating Conditions:** 28 students; individual, partner, and small-group routines; black-and-white paper printouts, pencils, rulers, standard grid paper, basic four-function/ordinary calculators, and a single teacher projector. No 1:1 student devices or laboratory hardware required.

## 1. Unit Narrative and Representational Progression

### 1.1 The Developmental Trajectory of Unit 5 (14-Period Streamlined Architecture)
Unit 5 serves as the vital bridge in Grade 8 mathematics from structural function definitions (Unit 4: 8.F.A) to applied mathematical modeling (8.F.B) and simultaneous linear systems (Unit 6: 8.EE.C.8). In Unit 4, students established that a function assigns to each input exactly one output, and that linear functions are governed by the equation $y = mx + b$, producing straight-line graphs. 

In this revised 14-period candidate, the unit progression has been streamlined to maximize net learning utility, minimize instructional friction, and eliminate topical fragmentation, while preserving 100% of the mathematical substance required by 8.F.B.4 and 8.F.B.5. The progression unfolds across five coherent instructional clusters:

1. **Cluster 1: Foundations of Rate of Change and Initial Value (Periods 1–4):**  
   Students confront the cognitive shift from Grade 7 proportional relationships ($y = kx$) to affine linear models ($y = mx + b$). The primary hurdle is dismantling the habit of computing $\frac{y}{x}$ rather than the difference quotient $\frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}$. Students explore what the initial value $b$ represents physically and graphically when $x = 0$, and construct initial equations from verbal scenarios.
2. **Cluster 2: Constructing Linear Models Across Multiple Representations (Periods 5–9):**  
   Students systematically learn to construct $y = mx + b$ across four major representational formats:
   - Tables with unit steps ($\Delta x = 1$) [Period 5]
   - Tables with non-consecutive inputs ($\Delta x > 1$) [Period 6 / Expanded Lesson 1]
   - Contextual word problems and physical systems (Filling & Draining tanks) [Period 7 / Expanded Lesson 2]
   - Coordinate graphs with scaled axes (overcoming the visual "counting grid squares" trap) [Period 8 / Expanded Lesson 3]
   - General coordinate point pairs $(x_1, y_1)$ and $(x_2, y_2)$ using algebraic solving for $b$ [Period 9].
3. **Cluster 3: Mid-Unit Synthesis & Contextual Precision (Period 10):**  
   A high-efficiency synthesis period that combines a 25-minute targeted 3-item formative assessment with a 25-minute structured precision discourse routine (MP2, MP6), requiring students to write rigorous contextual interpretations specifying compound units for both parameters.
4. **Cluster 4: Qualitative Analysis and Sketching of Functions (Periods 11–12, 8.F.B.5):**  
   The mathematical lens shifts from algebraic computation to qualitative curve reading and sketching:
   - Period 11 unifies qualitative analysis across both direction (increasing, decreasing, constant) and rate linearity (constant rate/straight line vs. changing rate/curve).
   - Period 12 unifies qualitative modeling by having students sketch continuous curves from narratives and immediately test/critique them in a collaborative card-matching lab (MP3, MP4).
5. **Cluster 5: Integration, Evaluation, and Bridge (Periods 13–14):**  
   Students take a comprehensive 50-minute summative assessment sampling core targets (Period 13), followed by an assessment debrief and a preview problem where two linear functions intersect, establishing the immediate entry condition for Unit 6 (Systems of Equations) [Period 14].

## 3. Pacing, Resource Feasibility, and Strategic Buffer Relationship

### 3.1 Pacing Realism within 50-Minute Periods
Every period in Unit 5 is structured according to a consistent four-phase instructional architecture designed for the 28-student classroom condition:
- **Phase 1: Retrieval Warm-Up (7–8 minutes):** Low-stakes spiral review of previous skills (e.g., calculating differences, plotting points, solving 1-step equations).
- **Phase 2: Launch & Collaborative Exploration (22–25 minutes):** Direct framing by teacher projection, followed by paired or small-group investigation using printed task sheets and rulers/calculators.
- **Phase 3: Structured Whole-Class Synthesis (10–12 minutes):** Teacher-orchestrated discussion comparing student methods and formalizing definitions.
- **Phase 4: Independent Check for Understanding / Cool-Down (5–6 minutes):** 1–2 focused questions completed individually to provide immediate diagnostic data for the teacher.

### 3.2 Distinction Between Core 14 Periods and Annual Buffer Reserve
- **The Core 14 Periods:** All 14 periods detailed above represent the complete, autonomous instructional plan for Unit 5. Every required learning target of 8.F.B.4 and 8.F.B.5 is addressed and assessed within this window. No homework is required to make up for in-class learning.
- **The 20-Period Annual Flex Reserve:** The blueprint allocates 20 flexible periods across the year (in four 5-period blocks). If a district calendar experiences severe disruptions (e.g., inclement weather, school assemblies) or if the Period 10 mid-unit assessment reveals widespread foundational deficits in rational number arithmetic, the teacher may utilize a session from the annual buffer reserve. However, Unit 5 does not require or assume extra periods to complete its core curriculum.

## 4. Preservation of the Expanded Three-Lesson Sequence (Periods 6, 7, 8)

The three consecutive lessons selected for deep delivery—**Periods 6, 7, and 8**—remain the central anchor of standard 8.F.B.4 in this revised candidate:
1. **Lesson 6 (Numerical/Tabular Representation):** Moves students beyond simple $+1$ counting patterns by presenting irregular tables where $\Delta x > 1$ and $x = 0$ is absent. It forces students to construct the difference quotient $\frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}$ and algebraically/numerically extrapolate to find the initial value $b$.
2. **Lesson 7 (Contextual/Applied Modeling):** Takes the mathematical mechanics of Lesson 6 and contextualizes them within a realistic physical system (the Water Storage Reservoir, featuring Tank A filling and Tank B draining). Students confront positive and negative rates of change, physical units, and realistic domain constraints ($V \ge 0$).
3. **Lesson 8 (Visual/Graphical Representation):** Translates the contextual and tabular models into the coordinate plane. It directly tackles the classic pedagogical pitfall where students count visual grid squares on graphs with unequal axis scales rather than computing true coordinate differences $\frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}$.

Because Periods 1–5 were preserved as a robust runway, Lessons 6, 7, and 8 maintain their exact numbering, task identifiers (`TASK-6.1` to `PRAC-8`), and student-facing materials without structural disruption.

## 5. Curricular Efficiency and Consolidation Rationale

To achieve the 20% budget reduction ($\lfloor 18 \times 0.8 \rfloor = 14$ periods) while safeguarding learning outcomes, instructional time was reorganized to maximize educational benefit per minute:

1. **Immediate Diagnostic-Feedback Integration (Period 10):**  
   In the 18-period version, Period 10 was dedicated entirely to taking an assessment and Period 11 to writing contextual sentences with units. Separating testing from feedback incurs a latency cost: students forget their immediate reasoning. Merging a compact 25-minute diagnostic with a 25-minute targeted discourse routine on precision and units (MP2, MP6) creates immediate corrective utility, transforming assessment into an active learning event.
2. **Holistic Qualitative Curve Analysis (Period 11):**  
   In the 18-period version, qualitative reading was divided across two separate days: direction (increasing/decreasing/constant) in P12 and linearity (linear vs. nonlinear) in P13. In authentic mathematical modeling, direction and curvature are evaluated simultaneously on the same curve. Unifying them in Period 11 reduces topical fragmentation and deepens cognitive engagement without adding student fatigue.
3. **Synergistic Qualitative Modeling (Period 12):**  
   Sketching from narratives (P14) and matching cards (P15) were previously isolated. In Period 12, students sketch their own graph from a narrative prompt and immediately test their thinking by matching and critiquing story and graph cards in pairs (MP3, MP4). This active synthesis provides immediate self-correction, producing greater learning gains than two passive, single-format days.
4. **Spaced Retrieval over Massed Cramming (Elimination of Standalone Review P16):**  
   An isolated pre-test review day (old P16) yields lower long-term retention than distributed, spaced retrieval. By incorporating cumulative spiral retrieval warm-ups across all 14 periods, students maintain active recall of earlier concepts continuously, rendering a dedicated cram day redundant and freeing valuable time for deep modeling.
