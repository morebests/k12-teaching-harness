## 2. Pedagogical Rationale and Curricular Trade-Offs

### 2.1 Maximizing Net Educational Benefit per Minute
From an instructional efficiency perspective, an optimal curriculum maximizes the net mathematical competence, conceptual clarity, and flourishing of all 28 students within the available time envelope. Simply compressing every lesson uniformly by 20% would create instructional rush, cognitive overload, and fragile procedural mimicry. 

Instead, this revision evaluated the marginal educational utility of each component across the 18-period arc:
- **High-Utility Core (Protected):** The foundational conceptual transition from Grade 7 proportional reasoning ($y/x$) to affine models ($y = mx + b$, $\Delta y / \Delta x$) requires unhurried, multi-representational investigation. Rushing Periods 1–5 would undermine student success across the entire unit. Likewise, the three expanded lessons (Lessons 6, 7, 8) form an essential representational triad (Table $\to$ Context $\to$ Scaled Graph) that grounds 8.F.B.4. These were protected in full.
- **Consolidation of Low-Marginal-Utility & Redundant Structures:** Four specific junctures were identified where instructional time was fragmented, separated by artificial day breaks, or reliant on passive review. Merging these junctures eliminated 4 periods while actually *improving* pedagogical coherence.

### 2.2 Detailed Breakdown of the Four Consolidations

#### Consolidation 1: Merging Assessment and Precision Discourse (Period 10)
- **Previous Structure:** Period 10 was an isolated 30-minute test with 20 minutes of peer review; Period 11 was an entire 50-minute lesson on writing sentences with compound units.
- **Revised Structure:** Merged into **Period 10: Mid-Unit Checkpoint & Contextual Parameter Precision**.
  - Minutes 0–25: Students independently complete a focused 3-item checkpoint evaluating the three core representations of 8.F.B.4 (an irregular table, a scaled coordinate graph, and a real-world scenario).
  - Minutes 25–50: The teacher uses immediate diagnostic observations to conduct a targeted whole-class synthesis, using structured sentence frames (*"For each 1 [input unit] increase, [output quantity] changes by [rate] [output units]"*) to solidify precision with physical units (MP2, MP6).
- **Educational Benefit:** Eliminates feedback latency. In cognitive science, immediate targeted feedback during the same class period produces significantly higher learning retention than delayed review.
- **Periods Saved:** 1 period.

#### Consolidation 2: Holistic Qualitative Curve Analysis (Period 11)
- **Previous Structure:** Period 12 analyzed increasing/decreasing/constant intervals; Period 13 analyzed linear (straight) vs. nonlinear (curved) motion behaviors.
- **Revised Structure:** Merged into **Period 11: Qualitative Graph Analysis: Direction, Constancy, and Linearity**.
  - Students analyze non-numeric curves holistically, characterizing both the direction of change and the rate linearity (e.g., distinguishing steady speed from acceleration on distance-time and speed-time graphs).
- **Educational Benefit:** Direction and curvature are simultaneous attributes of the same mathematical curve. Analyzing them together prevents artificial topical silos and strengthens student ability to interpret graphs qualitatively (8.F.B.5).
- **Periods Saved:** 1 period.

#### Consolidation 3: Active Qualitative Modeling & Card-Matching Lab (Period 12)
- **Previous Structure:** Period 14 had students sketch graphs from stories; Period 15 was a partner card-sort matching stories to pre-printed graphs.
- **Revised Structure:** Merged into **Period 12: Qualitative Modeling: Sketching and Narrative Matching Lab**.
  - Minutes 0–20: Students read verbal narratives and sketch their own qualitative distance-time curves.
  - Minutes 20–50: Pairs immediately compare their sketches against a deck of 6 commute stories, 6 graphs, and 6 written justification statements, engaging in active peer critique (MP3, MP4).
- **Educational Benefit:** Pairing generative sketching with comparative matching creates an immediate self-correcting feedback loop. Students actively evaluate differences between their sketches and formal graph representations.
- **Periods Saved:** 1 period.

#### Consolidation 4: Spaced Retrieval over Massed Cramming (Elimination of Standalone Review P16)
- **Previous Structure:** Period 16 was a standalone pre-assessment four-station review day immediately before the Period 17 exam.
- **Revised Structure:** Eliminated standalone review day; students transition directly from Period 12 qualitative modeling to Period 13 summative assessment.
- **Educational Benefit:** Cognitive psychology research demonstrates that distributed, spaced retrieval produces superior long-term retention compared to massed pre-exam cramming. Cumulative 7–8 minute spiral retrieval warm-ups are embedded in every lesson, ensuring active recall is maintained continuously throughout the unit.
- **Periods Saved:** 1 period.

**Total Net Savings:** $1 + 1 + 1 + 1 = 4$ periods ($18 - 4 = 14$ periods).

## 3. Preservation of Preceding Runway and Expanded Lessons

### 3.1 Protection of Periods 1–5
Periods 1–5 remain intact as five deliberate instructional steps:
- **P1:** Diagnostic and Proportional vs. Affine Linear Review ($y = kx$ vs. $y = mx + b$).
- **P2:** Defining Rate of Change vs. Coordinate Ratio (dismantling the $y/x$ habit).
- **P3:** Identifying and Extrapolating the Initial Value ($b$ when $x = 0$ is absent).
- **P4:** Constructing $y = mx + b$ from Verbal Narratives (distinguishing fixed fee from rate).
- **P5:** Linear Functions from Unit Tables ($\Delta x = 1$).

This protects students from premature cognitive overload before they encounter complex, irregular representations.
